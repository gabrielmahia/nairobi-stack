# Futures Lens — metabolizing accelerative change without the shock
**Location:** `nairobi-stack/docs/FUTURES_LENS.md`
**Purpose:** a standing filter for futurist commentary (podcasts, newsletters,
manifestos) so each new take is metabolized into doctrine or discarded — instead
of spawning fresh strategy documents. Method is Toffler's: track the *rate* of
change and the *collision of waves*, not the artifacts of change.

## 1. The intake filter (apply to any futurist claim)

1. **Separate structure from anecdote.** Orchestration-shift claims are structural
   (Probable, industry-confirmed). "$50M engineers" and "AGI arrived in March" are
   anecdotes and definition disputes — note, never build.
2. **Ask the Toffler question:** does this claim describe faster *technology* or
   faster *required human adaptation*? Only the second changes our roadmap.
3. **Ask the Baobab question:** if true, does it strengthen the railroad or merely
   describe a shinier train? Model-capability claims are trains by definition.
4. **Check whose wave.** A claim true in Palo Alto's pure third-wave economy may
   invert in a wave-collision economy. Test every claim against a county clerk in
   Machakos before adopting it.

## 2. Five mappings that survive the filter (July 2026 intake: Andreessen/Rogan)

**Future shock is the product risk.** "AGI arrived and nobody noticed" is the
textbook symptom: change velocity exceeding registration. For East Africa the
danger is not missing the technology — it is importing the shock without shock
absorbers. Rails that translate accelerating model capability into stable,
legible institutional interfaces ARE the shock absorber. Interface stability
while engines churn is the whole value proposition.

**Wave collision is our actual terrain.** The region runs first-wave agriculture,
second-wave paper bureaucracy, and third-wave mobile money simultaneously.
The leapfrog thesis (G3) is precisely a wave-skip claim: paper registries →
agent-mediated coordination without the full industrial intermediation layer.
M-Pesa proved wave-skipping once. G3 exists to test whether it generalizes —
which is why the pilot must measure **human adaptation load** (time-to-competence,
attention demanded, error recovery by non-specialists), not just task success.

**"One person, many agents" is Toffler's prosumer at endpoint** — the individual
as institution. But orchestration is only real where agents have rails to grip:
payments, registries, language grounding. Adhocracy needs a substrate; the bus
is one.

**Powershift, applied.** Toffler ranked power sources: violence (lowest quality),
wealth, knowledge (highest — infinitely shareable, hardest to monopolize).
Open weights plus open rails deliberately make the highest-quality power
non-excludable for the region. That is the political meaning of MIT licensing,
stated once here and nowhere publicly.

**The AI vampire is overchoice wearing a new coat.** Every productivity wave
raised expectations instead of reducing work. Standing design rule, already
encoded as the <120-second utility requirement, now generalized: every
user-facing tool must CLOSE loops (deliver a decision, a document, a
confirmation) rather than open them (dashboards, feeds, notifications).
Attention is the user's scarcest resource; systems that consume it net-negative
are vampires regardless of capability.

## 3. Standing disagreements (held until evidence moves)

The orchestration futurists systematically underweight three bottlenecks, and
all three are where this portfolio lives: **trust** (institutions do not automate
high-stakes decisions because capability exists — certification and DEMO/REAL
discipline are the unlock), **data** (agents are only as good as the registries
they can ground on — the dataset work), and **coordination** (most real failures
are incentive and interface failures, not reasoning failures — the bus). Better
models make these bottlenecks MORE binding, not less: engines improve for free;
rails, trust, and data improve only when someone builds them.

## 4. Skills doctrine (G6 alignment)

The scarce skill is not prompting; it is Toffler's literacy — learn, unlearn,
relearn — applied to systems: framing problems, setting objectives, evaluating
outputs, exercising judgment under uncertainty. The curriculum kit (G6) teaches
maintainers, not tool users, and optimizes for unlearning speed over tool
fluency, because the tools will churn beneath every cohort.
